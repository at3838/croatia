The eight days

AboutA 17th-century gate and the main land entrance into Trogir Old Town from the mainland side; starting here, the stone lanes lead naturally into the main square.

Main square (Trg Ivana Pavla II)

AboutThe civic core of the old town, ringed by St. Lawrence's Cathedral, the Clock Tower, City Hall and the City Loggia; the square's layout reflects Trogir's religious and public life as a self-governing medieval town. Stand in the middle and take in all four facades first, then look at the stone carving building by building.

Hours / costOutdoor square, open 24 hours, free.

Time needed20–30 minutes; most atmospheric at dusk when the lights come on.

Radovan's Portal · outside St. Lawrence's Cathedral

AboutThe portal was carved and signed by Master Radovan himself in 1240; he is regarded as one of the finest stonemasons of his time, and the work is a masterpiece of Croatian Romanesque carving. Adam and Eve stand on lions on either side of the doorway, the lintel tells the life of Jesus, and the columns carry reliefs of the twelve months of farm work.

How to see itThe portal is outside the main door of St. Lawrence's Cathedral — you can see it without buying a ticket or entering the cathedral; work from the bottom up, layer by layer, through the figures, animals and monthly scenes.

Hours / costRadovan's Portal is outside the cathedral's main door, viewable outdoors 24 hours, free; St. Lawrence's Cathedral charges for entry — we are not going in this time.

Time needed20–30 minutes.

Clock Tower + Chapel of St. Sebastian (Crkva sv. Sebastijan)

AboutThe Chapel of St. Sebastian was built in 1476 by Niccolò di Giovanni Fiorentino, a votive church raised by the townspeople in thanks for the plague receding; its Renaissance facade carries figures of St. Sebastian and Christ. The clock tower rises above the chapel and is one of the main square's most recognisable landmarks.

Hours / costThe Chapel of St. Sebastian is free to enter and we will go in; the clock tower exterior is free to admire from the main square.

Time needed10–15 minutes.

Cipiko Palace

AboutThe 15th-century palace of Coriolan Cippico, wealthy merchant and military commander, formed by merging several houses; the facade keeps Venetian Gothic trefoil windows and Renaissance stone carving — best studied from the main square.

Hours / costThe interior is not open to the public; the exterior can be viewed 24 hours, free.

Time needed10–15 minutes.

Kamerlengo Fortress

AboutA harbour fortress built up by the Venetians after they took over in the 15th century, its thick walls, corner towers and courtyard controlling the shipping channel. From the walls you can look back over the whole old town from the west, and over the yachts moored along the Trogir Promenade.

Key point59 m high, about 200 steps; first stop this morning — buy the Yellow ticket, then climb. Fewer people in the morning and less crowded stairs; with the light behind you, you can look over the Peristyle, Split harbour, Marjan hill and the red rooftops.

Peristyle

AboutThe ceremonial courtyard of Diocletian's Palace when it was completed around AD 305, its deep-red granite columns shipped in from Egypt; standing in the centre, you can take in the axis of the mausoleum, the great vestibule and the underground palace in one view.

Hours / costOutdoors, open 24 hours, free.

Time needed15–20 minutes.

Saint Domnius Cathedral

AboutOriginally the mausoleum of the Roman emperor Diocletian, converted into a church in the 7th century; the circular interior keeps its Roman colonnades, and you can also see medieval wooden doors, the altar and saints' relics.

Hours / costCurrent information says it opens from 08:15; the Yellow ticket, about €13 (price on site), includes the cathedral.

Baptistery / Temple of Jupiter

AboutOriginally a Roman temple to Jupiter within the palace, converted into a baptistery in the Middle Ages; don't miss the complete barrel-vaulted coffered ceiling, the ancient Egyptian sphinx outside the door and the 13th-century stone baptismal font.

Hours / costCurrent information says it opens from 08:15; the Yellow ticket, about €13 (price on site), includes the Baptistery / Temple of Jupiter.

Plitvice Route C + Zadar sunset

Plitvice → Zadar · about 133 km / 1 hour 50 minutes (normal October conditions) · motorway toll about €4.10 (Gornja Ploča → Zadar Istok; HAC Category I car)

Route C starts at Entrance 1, drops down into the Lower Lakes Canyon, then takes in the Great Waterfall (Veliki Slap), crosses Lake Kozjak by boat to the Upper Lakes boardwalks, and finishes with the panoramic train back to the entrance. The lake colours shift with minerals, light and organic matter; the boardwalks are slippery when wet, so stop walking before you reach for the camera. Leave the park early today — be in Zadar before 17:30 to make the 18:01 sunset.

Be in Zadar before 17:30

The 18:01 sunset is a hard deadline: be driving out of Plitvice by about 15:15. If traffic or park transport is delayed, cut the Upper Lakes short — don't try to make up time on the road before sunset.

Plitvice Lakes National Park

AboutSixteen lakes linked by natural travertine dams, the water stepping down into waterfalls and emerald pools; listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1979. Route C is about 8 km and strings together the Lower Lakes Canyon, the park's tallest waterfall, the Great Waterfall (Veliki Slap), Lake Kozjak and the forested Upper Lakes in one go.

TipsTake in the whole canyon from the Entrance 1 viewpoint first, then go down to the boardwalks; the water at the bends of the Upper Lakes is calmer and better for reflections. You must stay on the marked routes — no swimming and no drones; wear grippy shoes and bring a light rain jacket.

Opening hoursEntrance 1 ticket sales 07:00–15:00; electric boat 08:00–16:30, panoramic train 08:00–17:30 (every 30 minutes) — timetables on site prevail.

AdmissionAdult one-day ticket €23 (October price; includes the Lake Kozjak electric boat and the park panoramic train).

Time neededRoute C takes 6–8 hours; with a slower walker in the party, plan generously with extra rests, plus 30 minutes waiting for the boat / train.

Sea Organ + The Greeting to the Sun

Sunset planBe at the waterfront before 17:30, sit down and listen to the waves driving the pipes, and watch the Adriatic sunset at 18:01; after sunset, The Greeting to the Sun shows up much more strongly.

Hours / costOutdoor public space, open 24 hours, free.

Parking nearby: park at P1 (by Entrance 1, ticket office just across the road); €1.50/hour, not included in the park ticket. Best to arrive between 08:30 and 09:00.

Zadar Old Town, the city walls and The Gold and Silver of Zadar exhibition

Zadar → Kaštel Lukšić · about 125 km / 1 hour 35 minutes (normal October conditions) · motorway toll about €8.10 (Zadar Istok → Dugopolje; HAC Category I car)

Keep the whole morning for Zadar: follow the UNESCO Venetian Works of Defence from the Land Gate to Five Wells Square, then through the Roman Forum, churches and the monastery. After lunch, head straight south to Kaštel Lukšić — no driving into the night.

Clock change to winter time

25 October is the day Croatia switches to winter time (DST ends) and the clocks go back 1 hour; from then on, sunrise is about 06:26 and sunset about 16:51.

D8 / A1 safety cut-off: 14:30

The D8 coastal road is Croatia's most beautiful scenic drive, but it's slow and winding. If you linger too long in Zadar and only set off in the afternoon on the D8, you'll run into the 17:45 darkness — and after the clock change, sunset is even earlier. Leave Zadar by 14:30 at the latest. If time is short, don't force the full D8: take the A1 motorway quickly south first, then cut back to the coast near Kaštel, so you're never driving unlit coastal cliff roads in the dark.

Road notes

Driving essentials

Driving on the right, tolled motorways, city parking and short daylight are the four big things on this trip. And don't stray into the Dubrovnik Old Town gates — cameras enforce them automatically.

95

Fill up with Eurosuper

Our notes put fuel at about €1.76/litre; a 50-litre fill is about €88. The return is full-to-full, so top up near the airport at the end.

50

Limits start in town

50 in built-up areas, 90 outside, 110 on expressways, 130 km/h on motorways; drive on the right, and give way to traffic already on the roundabout.

€

Motorways charge by distance

Pay in euros cash or by card at the exit; references: Split–Zadar about €5.10, Split–Skradin about €2.80.

P

Don't park carelessly in centres

Use a parking meter or a phone app — badly parked cars can be towed. In Zadar and Makarska, park on the edge and walk in.

112

Emergency number

Croatia's general emergency number is 112. Per our notes, a UK licence needs no international driving permit.

Oct

Short days, rainy season

Start scenic stretches and national parks early; Dubrovnik gets about 120 mm of rain in October — check the weather again before departure.

One last thing before departure

The Plitvice tickets are bought: 24 October, 09:00, Entrance 1, 3 tickets. Only Buža Bar's opening that week remains to check — then the whole route is ready to follow with confidence.
